Implementation
--------------

Each velocity iteration previously computed both bodies' contact velocities
before the normal solve, applied the normal impulse, and then computed both
contact velocities again for friction.

Preparation now caches how a unit normal impulse changes relative contact
velocity along each tangent. The iteration derives both post-normal tangent
velocities from the already-computed pre-normal relative velocity and the
applied normal impulse. It avoids two cross products and the second set of
body-velocity reads per visited point. Iteration count, contact order,
Gauss-Seidel updates, effective masses, and impulse limits are unchanged.

Each prepared contact point grows by two floats.

The algebra is equivalent, but deriving the post-normal velocity changes
floating-point operation order. Small local differences accumulate into
different long-run trajectories, as expected for this contact-dense scene.
No solver tolerance, iteration count, or physical limit changed.

Decision
--------

Retain. The experiment exceeds the predeclared gates of 10% lower
velocity-iteration time and 3% lower full-step time at both target sizes.
It also improves the settled-box control while preserving its stationary
state and exact contact load.
