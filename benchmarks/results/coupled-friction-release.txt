Implementation
--------------

The previous solver handled two orthogonal friction axes sequentially. Each
axis reread both bodies' contact velocities, clamped its accumulated impulse
independently, and updated both bodies before the next axis.

The experiment precomputes and inverts the coupled 2x2 tangent effective-mass
matrix. Each point and iteration now:

1. Reads one post-normal relative velocity.
2. Solves both tangent impulse components together.
3. Projects the accumulated vector onto the circular Coulomb limit.
4. Applies one combined tangent impulse to each body.

Warm-started tangent impulses use the same circular limit.

Behavior
--------

This intentionally changes friction from independent per-axis clamping, whose
combined magnitude can reach sqrt(2) times the intended limit, to a circular
Coulomb limit. The deterministic scene therefore follows a different
trajectory:

Both variants completed with valid finite states and no escaped or below-floor
bodies. A 512-box settled-stack workload remained stationary with exactly 512
manifolds and 2,048 points. Its mean step time improved from 1.0492 to
0.7794 ms (25.7%), while the fully sleeping control remained effectively flat
at 0.2405 versus 0.2421 ms.

Decision
--------

Retain for review. The change directly addresses the measured hot loop and
exceeds a 5% full-step improvement at both 5,000 and 10,000 bodies. It also
uses a rotationally symmetric Coulomb limit instead of the prior square
friction bound. The intentional trajectory change means correctness should be
judged by friction invariants and stability, not exact parity with the old
axis-order-dependent result.
