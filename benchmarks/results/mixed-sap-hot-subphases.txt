Instrumentation
---------------

The solver records four timers per step: constraint preparation, warm start,
eight fixed velocity iterations, and contact-cache update. Narrowphase records
candidate and successful-contact counts for sphere/sphere, sphere/box, and
box/box pairs. Per-candidate timers were deliberately avoided because their
clock overhead would distort the hot loop.

The dedicated CPU profiling runner now supports the same mixed 5,000- and
10,000-body SAP scenes. It does not use the allocation-counting override or
retain per-step timing samples. Raw machine-specific call graphs are retained
as session artifacts rather than repository results.

Top-level timing
----------------

bodies,step_ms,broadphase_ms,narrowphase_ms,solver_ms
5000,9.14,2.16,1.92,4.97
10000,19.79,5.35,3.91,10.37

The corresponding uninstrumented main medians were 9.15 and 19.72 ms. The
instrumented means differ by -0.1% and +0.4%, which is below run-to-run noise
in these captures.

The instrumented 10,000-body profile attributes 52.1% of main-thread samples
to the solver, 27.2% to SAP, and 16.2% to narrowphase. The corresponding
5,000-body shares are 54.6%, 23.0%, and 17.8%. These agree with the explicit
stage timers.

Solver
------

bodies,prepare_ms,warm_start_ms,velocity_iterations_ms,cache_update_ms,prepared_points,velocity_point_visits
5000,0.5267,0.4802,3.8496,0.1119,11982.82,95862.57
10000,1.1511,1.0365,7.9535,0.2306,23810.79,190486.36

The fixed eight velocity sweeps are the dominant measured region: 42.1% of the
entire 5,000-body step and 40.2% of the 10,000-body step. Preparation, warm
start, and cache update together account for only about 12% of either step.

Within the sampled velocity-loop region, source-attributed samples were:

bodies,normal_lines,friction_lines,optimized_or_shared_lines
5000,31.0%,40.2%,28.8%
10000,28.6%,42.7%,28.7%

Optimized source-line attribution is approximate, but both captures identify
the two-tangent friction section as the largest named part of the velocity
loop. The current loop rereads both bodies' contact velocities and applies
body updates independently for the normal and each of two tangent axes.

Broadphase
----------

5000,0.0113,0.1833,1.4650,0.4607
10000,0.0229,0.3881,3.8475,1.0093

At 10,000 bodies, sweep-and-emit alone costs 19.5% of the complete step and
processes about 1.03 million X-window comparisons to emit about 24,682
candidates. Record construction and initial sorting are not meaningful targets.

Box/box tests are only 34.6% of candidates but consume about 80% of sampled
narrowphase time. Because sphere contacts have one point, box/box manifolds
account for the remaining 7,108.31 points at 5,000 bodies and 13,994.45 points
at 10,000 bodies: about 59% of all solver points.

Conclusion
----------

The primary bottleneck is the fixed velocity solver's per-contact-point loop,
not allocation, cache lookup, transform composition, or convergence checking.
Its two-axis friction work is the largest identifiable portion. The strongest
next experiment is a combined two-dimensional friction solve that computes one
post-normal relative velocity and applies one combined tangent impulse, with
strict trajectory, stability, and full-step performance gates.

The secondary targets are box/box manifold generation and SAP's X-window
sweep. Reducing box contact points may also lower solver work, but bluntly
capping manifolds would trade away stack stability and is not justified by
these measurements alone.
