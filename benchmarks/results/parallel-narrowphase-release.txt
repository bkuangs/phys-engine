Implementation
--------------

Each world can opt into a worker count that includes the calling thread. A
count of one preserves serial execution. Larger counts use world-owned
persistent threads and reusable per-candidate result slots. Workers claim
fixed-size chunks, write only to their candidate's slot, and never mutate body
or solver state. The calling thread then compacts successful contacts in the
original candidate order.

The parallel path starts at 4,096 candidate pairs. Smaller scenes stay serial
even when more workers are configured. Copying a world copies the configured
count but not transient pool state; moving a world transfers the workspace and
its pool safely.

Tests also compare every manifold's body handles, normal, point count, world
points, local anchors, and penetration depth between serial and parallel
execution. ThreadSanitizer reports no races in the exercised worker-pool,
world-copy, world-move, reconfiguration, and shutdown paths.

Decision
--------

Retain as an opt-in feature. Two and four workers comfortably exceed the gates
of 30% lower narrowphase time, 7% lower 10,000-body full-step time, no
meaningful small-scene regression, and exact output. The default remains one
worker to avoid implicit thread creation and oversubscription when applications
run multiple worlds.
